If you leave most calls with a page of half-written notes, Otter.ai is built for you. It joins the meeting, types out what everyone says and hands you a summary with the action items pulled out. In this Otter.ai review we go through the plans, the limits that catch people out on the free tier, and the cases where another tool makes more sense. What is Otter.ai?

Otter writes the meeting down while it happens and labels each speaker, so you can scroll back and see who said what. An AI Meeting Agent can join your Zoom, Microsoft Teams and Google Meet calls automatically from your calendar, so you don't have to remember to press record. If you don't want a bot in the call, the Mac and Windows desktop apps can record straight from your computer.

After the call, Otter writes a summary with decisions, key points and action items. Otter AI Chat then lets you ask questions across your meetings and connected apps. For example, you could ask what the team agreed about a deadline, and then turn the answer into a follow-up email or report.

Key features of Otter.ai

Live transcription with speaker labels

Text appears while people talk and is split by speaker. Otter's pricing page lists AI transcription in English, Spanish, French, German, Japanese and Chinese, plus custom vocabulary for names and terms (5 terms on the free plan).

Automatic summaries and action items

Every plan gets an automatic summary with an outline and the action items. So you can skim a few lines instead of scrolling the whole transcript.

Otter AI Chat

AI Chat works within a single meeting and across all of them. It has monthly limits: 20 questions per user on Basic (3 per conversation), 50 on Pro and 200 on Business and Enterprise.

Integrations

The homepage names Slack, Salesforce, HubSpot, Jira, Notion, Asana, Google Docs and Dropbox among its connected apps, and the Pro plan lists Salesforce, HubSpot and Zapier. Action items often end up on a task board. If that's how your team works, our Trello review explains how a simple board tool can take over from there.

Apps

There are Mac and Windows apps, iOS and Android apps and a Chrome extension. You can also record from the browser.

Otter.ai free plan: what you actually get

The Basic plan is free forever and includes 300 transcription minutes a month, 20 AI Chat questions and the Zoom, Teams and Meet integrations. Its limits are tight:

  • Each conversation stops at 30 minutes.
  • You can import only 3 audio or video files in total, ever.
  • Only your 25 most recent conversations stay in your history.
  • You can join only one meeting at a time.

Fine for stand-ups and for trying Otter on a real call. If your meetings run an hour, you'll hit the cap every time and need Pro (90 minutes) or Business (4 hours).

Is Otter.ai worth paying for?

It depends mostly on how long your meetings are and what language they're in.

Stay on the free plan if your calls are short check-ins and you only want a transcript now and then. Record one real meeting first and see whether the summary is good enough to share.

Pay for Pro if you have hour-long calls most days. Pick yearly billing: it's $8.33 a month instead of $16.99. Pro also raises file imports from 3 in total to 10 a month, which matters if you upload recordings.

Look at Business if meetings run past 90 minutes, or a team of more than five needs to share one workspace.

Try something else if your team speaks a language outside English, Spanish, French, German, Japanese and Chinese, or if you need HIPAA, SSO or SCIM without going through an Enterprise sales call.

Our verdict

For most people in English-language meetings, Otter's Pro plan on yearly billing is the sensible pick: long enough meetings, enough minutes, and a price that's easy to justify. The free plan is a decent trial but the 30-minute cap gets in the way fast. If you're still deciding, see the other AI meeting tools we cover, or Otter's profile page for the raw plan details.
