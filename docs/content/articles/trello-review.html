Trello is a visual project management tool from Atlassian. You organise work as cards that move across lists on a board. This Trello review covers how it works, what the Free, Standard, Premium and Enterprise plans include and cost, and when a different tool fits better. We checked every price and limit on Trello's official website on 5 November 2026, following our review method.

  • Best for: freelancers and small teams who want a simple, visual way to track tasks.
  • Free plan: yes. It covers up to 10 collaborators and 10 boards per Workspace, with unlimited cards.
  • Paid plans: Standard is $5 and Premium is $10 per user/month with annual billing.
  • Watch out for: Timeline, Calendar and Table views are listed on Premium, and Trello is cloud-only.

What is Trello?

Trello is built around three ideas: boards, lists and cards. A board is one project. Lists are the stages of that project, such as To do, Doing and Done. Cards are the tasks, and they can have due dates, assignees, checklists and attachments. You drag a card from one list to the next as work moves forward.

The homepage also highlights Inbox, which captures to-dos from email, Slack and Microsoft Teams, and Planner, which syncs your calendar so you can block time for tasks. Automation, templates and Power-Ups are built on top of those boards.

Key features of Trello

Boards, lists and cards

Every plan includes unlimited cards and a full activity log. Standard adds Custom Fields, advanced checklists, list colours, collapsible lists and card mirroring, which shows one card on several boards.

Built-in automation

Trello says "powerful no-code automation is built into every Trello board". Automation is measured in command runs: 250 a month on Free, 1,000 on Standard and unlimited on Premium.

Power-Ups and integrations

Power-Ups connect Trello to more than 200 apps, such as Slack, Google Drive and Salesforce. The pricing page notes that some partner Power-Ups need their own paid subscription.

Views

Premium lists Calendar, Timeline, Table, Dashboard and Map views, plus Workspace-wide Table and Calendar views. These help when one board isn't enough to see everything going on.

AI features

AI is listed on Standard and Premium. The comparison table describes AI summarization, AI organization (generating checklists, adding due dates, merging cards and scheduling focus time) and AI-generated boards that you create by describing your goal.

How billing works. Trello counts every member added to a Workspace as a billable user. Any Workspace can start a free Premium trial. If you downgrade a paid Workspace, it becomes a free Workspace that can hold 10 boards.

Trello free plan: is it enough?

For a small team, often yes. The Free plan includes unlimited cards, unlimited Power-Ups per board, assignees and due dates, mobile apps and two-factor authentication. The limits to plan around are:

  • 10 collaborators per Workspace
  • 10 boards per Workspace
  • 10MB per uploaded file
  • 250 automation command runs a month

Once you need more boards or bigger files, Standard is the next step. Premium makes sense when you need timelines, calendars or dashboards across several projects.

How to get started with Trello

Trello's homepage sums up setup as: sign up, create a board, and you're off. In practice, a first project takes a few minutes:

  1. Create a free account on the web, or download the desktop or mobile app.
  2. Make one board for a single project, or start from a template.
  3. Add lists for your stages. To do, Doing and Done is enough to begin.
  4. Add cards for each task, then set due dates, assignees and checklists.
  5. Invite your team. Remember the free plan allows up to 10 collaborators per Workspace.
  6. Automate one step, such as moving a card when a checklist is complete, then add the Power-Ups you need.

Keep the first board simple. You can add Custom Fields, more automation or Premium views later, once you know what your team actually uses.

Trello pros and cons

Pros

  • A permanent free plan that covers a small team
  • Automation is included on every plan
  • Large library of Power-Ups for other tools
  • Apps for the web, iOS, Android and desktop

Cons

  • Free plan caps a Workspace at 10 collaborators and 10 boards
  • Timeline, Calendar and Table views are listed on Premium only
  • Cloud-only, with no self-hosted option
  • Some partner Power-Ups cost extra

Who should use Trello?

A good fit if you:

  • Want to see all your tasks at a glance without a long setup
  • Run a small team and can work within 10 boards for now
  • Already use Slack, Google Drive or Salesforce and want tasks connected to them

Look elsewhere if you:

  • Need Gantt-style timelines on a tight budget
  • Must host your project data on your own servers
  • Run heavy automation and can't afford Premium's unlimited command runs

Tips before you choose a plan

  1. Start free. Build one real project board and invite your team before you pay.
  2. Count your boards. If you'll have more than 10 active projects, budget for Standard.
  3. Check the views you need. If you need Timeline or Calendar, compare Premium's price with other tools.
  4. Pay annually if you're sure. Standard is $5 instead of $6, and Premium $10 instead of $12.50.
  5. Capture tasks where they start. If your to-dos come out of meetings, an AI note-taker can list them first. See our Otter.ai review.

Our verdict

On paper, Trello is one of the simplest ways to organise team work. You get boards anyone understands, automation on every plan and a free tier that really works for small teams. Standard removes the board limit for $5 per user/month, and Premium adds the extra views growing teams usually need.
