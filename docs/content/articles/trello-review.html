Trello is the board with sticky-note cards that you drag from "To do" to "Done". It's made by Atlassian, and it's one of the quickest ways to get a small team organised. This Trello review looks at what each plan adds, where the free plan stops, and when you'd be better off with something heavier.   • Best for: freelancers and small teams who want to see every task on one screen.
  • Free plan: 10 people, 10 boards, unlimited cards.
  • Paid plans: $5 (Standard) or $10 (Premium) per user/month, billed yearly.
  • Catch: timeline and calendar views need Premium.

What is Trello?

Everything in Trello is a board, a list or a card. A board is one project. Lists are the stages of that project, such as To do, Doing and Done. Cards are the tasks, and they can have due dates, assignees, checklists and attachments. You drag a card from one list to the next as work moves forward.

The homepage also highlights Inbox, which captures to-dos from email, Slack and Microsoft Teams, and Planner, which syncs your calendar so you can block time for tasks. Automation, templates and Power-Ups are built on top of those boards.

Key features of Trello

Boards, lists and cards

Every plan includes unlimited cards and a full activity log. Standard adds Custom Fields, advanced checklists, list colours, collapsible lists and card mirroring, which shows one card on several boards.

Built-in automation

You can set rules like "when a card moves to Done, tick the checklist and notify the owner". Each time a rule fires it uses a command run: 250 a month on Free, 1,000 on Standard and unlimited on Premium.

Power-Ups and integrations

Power-Ups connect Trello to more than 200 apps, such as Slack, Google Drive and Salesforce. The pricing page notes that some partner Power-Ups need their own paid subscription.

Views

Boards are great until you have five of them. Premium adds Calendar, Timeline, Table, Dashboard and Map views, plus Table and Calendar views across the whole workspace.

AI features

AI is listed on Standard and Premium. The comparison table describes AI summarization, AI organization (generating checklists, adding due dates, merging cards and scheduling focus time) and AI-generated boards that you create by describing your goal.

How billing works. Trello counts every member added to a Workspace as a billable user. Any Workspace can start a free Premium trial. If you downgrade a paid Workspace, it becomes a free Workspace that can hold 10 boards.

Trello free plan: is it enough?

For a small team, usually. You get unlimited cards and Power-Ups, due dates, assignees and the mobile apps. What you'll run into:

  • 10 collaborators per Workspace
  • 10 boards per Workspace
  • 10MB per uploaded file
  • 250 automation command runs a month

The board limit is the one most teams hit first.

How to get started with Trello

A first board takes about ten minutes:

  1. Create a free account on the web, or download the desktop or mobile app.
  2. Make one board for a single project, or start from a template.
  3. Add lists for your stages. To do, Doing and Done is enough to begin.
  4. Add cards for each task, then set due dates, assignees and checklists.
  5. Invite your team. Remember the free plan allows up to 10 collaborators per Workspace.
  6. Automate one step, such as moving a card when a checklist is complete, then add the Power-Ups you need.

Resist adding labels, custom fields and five automations on day one. Use the board for a week and add only what you miss.

Trello pros and cons

Pros

  • Free plan is enough for many small teams
  • Automation is included on every plan
  • Over 200 Power-Ups for other apps
  • Apps for the web, iOS, Android and desktop

Cons

  • Free plan caps a Workspace at 10 collaborators and 10 boards
  • Timeline, Calendar and Table views are listed on Premium only
  • Cloud-only, with no self-hosted option
  • Some partner Power-Ups cost extra

Trello is the wrong tool if you need proper Gantt charts on a small budget or have to host data on your own servers, because it's cloud-only. And if most of your tasks start life in meetings, pair it with a note-taker: our Otter.ai review covers one that pulls out action items for you.

Our verdict

Start on the free plan. Most small teams can run on it for months, and when the 10-board limit starts to pinch, Standard at $5 a user is an easy upgrade. Go to Premium only if you really need timelines or a cross-project dashboard; at $10 a user, that's where tools built for bigger projects start to look like better value.
